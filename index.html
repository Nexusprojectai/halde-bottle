Why it holds
when others don't.

  • Copper-lined vacuum gap

    Most bottles silver-coat the gap. Copper reflects heat roughly twice as well, which is the whole 24 hours right there.

  • Steel where it touches water

    The cap threads onto steel, not plastic. Nothing in the water path off-gasses, and nothing holds yesterday's taste.

  • Half-turn lid

    One short turn opens it. There's no click, no thread-hunting at 6 a.m. in the dark.

  • A base that doesn't wobble

    Machined flat, with a silicone ring recessed inside the wall — quiet on a desk, stable on a train table.

The questions
worth answering.

Will it fit in a cup holder?

The base is 7.2 cm across, which is inside the range most car and stroller cup holders accept. If your holder is tight on a standard 0.5 L bottle, it will be tight on this one too.

Does it taste like metal?

No. The interior is uncoated 18/8 stainless, the same alloy used in hospital and food-service equipment. Metal taste comes from older carbon-steel bottles or from soap left in the seal — both avoided here.

Can I put coffee in it?

Yes, and the lid seals well enough for it. Coffee will leave an aroma behind in the silicone seal after a few weeks; soaking the seal in hot water with a spoon of baking soda clears it.

How do I get the seal replaced?

Write to us with the date on the base. We send a new seal once a year, free, for as long as you own the bottle — no receipt, no proof of purchase.

What actually breaks first?

The seal, after roughly two years of daily use — which is why it's replaceable. The vacuum and the body are built to outlast the person carrying them.
